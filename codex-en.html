Table A · Daily Price Adjustment 每日价格调整判定表

When: every day. Inputs: ① forecast OCC of the target date (rooms on hand + forecast pickup ÷ available rooms); ② Pickup deviation = (current rooms on hand − same on-the-books last year at the same booking window) ÷ last year × 100%.

Rows = forecast OCC · Columns = Pickup deviation · Cells = price action (HOLD / ↑ / ↓)

Execution constraints (all five mandatory; a violation is an operating incident): ① cumulative same-direction change per day ≤ 20%; ≤ 2 same-direction changes within 24h. ② each change ≥ 3% (below 3% the market cannot feel it — it is noise, not pricing). ③ within 30 minutes after any change, verify rate parity across OTA listings. ④ changes touching a D1 peak date require L3 (Revenue Manager) approval. ⑤ log every change in the Rate Change Log, citing "per Table A".

Table C · ARI — Average Rate Index 价格指数判定表

Question answered: am I selling dearer or cheaper than the street? ARI = own ADR ÷ comp-set weighted ADR × 100. Benchmark ≈ 100 = at parity.

ARIReadingAction
≥ 115Significantly dearerJustify with product/reviews; watch volume (MPI) weekly
105–115DearerHold if MPI ≥ 100; otherwise audit value perception
95–105At parityHealthy. Maintain discipline
85–95CheaperTest ↑3–5% on low-demand days; watch pickup 48h
75–85Clearly cheaperStructural: fix rate ladder & channel mix before touching BAR
< 75Way cheaperEmergency pricing audit — you are subsidising the street
Common mistake: comparing your BAR against a competitor's selling rate including taxes/fees — always compare net-of-parity, same-room-class, same-date rates.

Table D · MPI — Market Penetration Index 市场渗透指数判定表

Question answered: am I capturing my fair share of this street's demand? MPI = own OCC ÷ comp-set OCC × 100. MPI > 100 = above fair share.

MPIReadingAction
≥ 110LeadingHarvest: protect rate (Table C), don't chase volume
100–110Above shareMaintain; document what works
90–100Near parityNormal noise; monitor weekly
80–90Below shareCheck visibility (OTA ranking, content) before cutting rate
< 80Share crisisRed-line review: product, reviews, channel presence, then price
Triangle diagnosis: RGI = MPI × ARI ÷ 100. Volume strong + price weak (MPI 96 / ARI 88) = a pricing-position problem, not a demand problem — the response is completely different from a share crisis. Never read one index alone.

Table E · Overbooking 超额预订标准表

Formula: oversell = available rooms × oversell rate (floor). Three steps: look up the base rate → apply the risk modifier → apply the hard cap.

No-show + 24h cancellation (last 30d)Base oversell rate
< 5%3%
5–8%5%
8–12%8%
12–15%10%
> 15%12% + audit the data

Risk modifiers: conservative ×0.4 · neutral ×0.6 · aggressive ×0.8. Hard cap: never exceed 5% of available rooms on any single day, regardless of statistics — the cap is the last line of defence against statistical failure.

Walked-guest protocol: walk cost (relocation + transport + apology amenity + review damage) typically exceeds the revenue of the extra room sold 2–3×. If a walk is unavoidable: same-tier or better hotel nearby, transport paid, first-night refunded, GM follow-up call within 24h.

Displacement test (for L3 review): group net contribution = group revenue − (transient rooms displaced × transient marginal rate) + F&B/meeting contribution. Accept only if net positive AND on-hand OCC < 85%. Full-service hotels: run Table K (banquet contribution) before rejecting a meeting group.

Table H · Channel Open / Close 渠道开关判定表

Principle: when rooms sell easily, close the expensive channels; when they don't, open everything. Status by forecast OCC:

≥ 95%CloseCloseOpenCloseOpen
85–95%Best-rate tiers onlyOpenOpenCloseOpen

Counter-intuitive truth: an OTA sale at ¥600 (net ≈ ¥510 after 15% commission) is worth less than a direct sale at ¥530. Compare channels on net contribution, never on gross rate. Rate-parity red lines: direct price must never sit above any OTA listing; any parity breach must be fixed within 30 minutes of a price change.

Common mistake: staring only at the near window. The far window (D–30/60/90) is where groups and events are won or lost — by the time it enters D–7, most of the revenue is already decided.

Table J · Complimentary Upgrades 免费升级规则表

Three legitimate triggers: ① oversell requires freeing a room; ② forecast vacancy of a high-tier type > 20%; ③ member benefit fulfilment. Upgrades are the cheapest satisfaction tool — but only under rules.

Hard limits: one tier per upgrade (no skipping two); never upgrade into a suite without L3 approval; upgrades are logged daily with reason codes; front desk may not upgrade "to be nice" outside the three triggers.

Table K · Meeting / Banquet Combined Revenue 会议／宴会综合收益联动表

Principle: the full-service hotel's superpower — rooms lost at the front can be earned back through F&B and function space. Meeting-group value = rooms + F&B + venue rental + AV/other.

Meeting scaleMinimum room-night commitmentF&B minimumDecision

Run Table G first for the room side; Table K decides whether the banquet contribution rescues a marginal room rate. Strategic value (annual conference rights, future pipeline) is logged separately — never blended into today's net contribution.

Table O · Priority & Conflict Arbitration 决策优先级与冲突仲裁表

When two tables disagree, this table wins. With it, a trainee is never stuck — it completes the "execute blindfolded" methodology.

PriorityTableScope
1Table L — DisruptionSafety and force majeure override everything
2Table E — Overbooking hard capAvailability safety beats revenue
3Table B — InventoryInventory actions outrank price actions
4Table A — PriceThe daily default
5Table H — ChannelsServes the price decision
6Table G/K — GroupsEvaluated within the frame set by 1–5
Three iron rules of conflict: ① safety beats revenue, always; ② inventory beats price (a room you shouldn't sell can't be fixed by a better price); ③ when still tied, escalate to L3 with the logs — never improvise.
